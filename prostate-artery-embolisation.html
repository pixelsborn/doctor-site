Understanding Prostate Enlargement (BPH)

Prostate enlargement, also known as Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH), is a common condition seen in older men. It is often referred to as an age-related urinary problem.

This condition can lead to symptoms such as:

  • Poor urine flow
  • Frequent urination
  • Incomplete emptying of the bladder
  • Sudden urge to urinate

While medications can help some patients, many individuals continue to experience symptoms. In such cases, surgical procedures like TURP (Transurethral Resection of the Prostate) are traditionally recommended.

However, modern medicine now offers a safer and minimally invasive alternative known as Prostate Artery Embolization (PAE).

What is Prostate Artery Embolization (PAE)?

Prostate Artery Embolization (PAE) is an advanced, minimally invasive procedure used to treat urinary symptoms caused by an enlarged prostate.

In this technique, tiny particles (microparticles) are injected into the blood vessels supplying the prostate. This reduces blood flow to the gland, causing it to shrink over time and relieve symptoms.

The procedure is performed by a highly trained Interventional Radiologist using real-time imaging guidance, without the need for open surgery.

PAE Procedure – Step by Step

The procedure is performed under imaging guidance such as X-ray or ultrasound, allowing precise and safe treatment without surgery.

  • A small incision is made in the wrist or groin area
  • A thin catheter is inserted into the artery
  • The catheter is guided to the prostate blood vessels
  • A contrast dye is injected to map the arteries
  • Microparticles are injected to block blood flow
  • Both right and left prostate arteries are treated
  • The catheter is removed and pressure is applied to prevent bleeding

The entire procedure is minimally invasive and typically completed within a short duration.

Following the procedure, the prostate gradually shrinks, and symptoms improve within days to weeks.
