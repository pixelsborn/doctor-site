What is Varicocele?

A varicocele is an abnormal enlargement of veins within the scrotum, similar to varicose veins seen in the legs. These veins carry blood away from the testicles, and when the valves fail, blood pools and causes swelling.

This condition affects nearly 15% of men and is commonly seen between the ages of 15 to 25 years. Most cases occur on the left side, though both sides can be affected in rare cases.

While many varicoceles are harmless, some may lead to complications such as reduced sperm production, poor sperm quality, or infertility.

Symptoms of Varicocele

Varicocele often shows no symptoms and is detected during routine check-ups. When symptoms occur, they may include:

  • Dull or sharp pain in the scrotum
  • Increased pain after standing or physical activity
  • Heavy feeling in the testicle
  • Swelling of the scrotum
  • Visible twisted veins ("bag of worms" appearance)
  • Reduced fertility or sperm quality

Causes of Varicocele

The exact cause of varicocele is not always clear, but it is often linked to faulty valves in the veins that regulate blood flow.

  • Defective vein valves causing blood backflow
  • Increased pressure in testicular veins
  • Poor blood circulation leading to vein enlargement

This condition is commonly diagnosed during puberty when the testicles grow rapidly and require increased blood flow.

Varicocele Treatment

Modern treatment focuses on minimally invasive techniques that provide effective results with faster recovery.

Varicocele Embolization

Varicocele embolization is an advanced, non-surgical procedure performed by an interventional radiologist.

  • A small puncture is made in the groin or wrist
  • A catheter is inserted into the affected vein
  • Coils or sclerosant agents are used to block the vein
  • Blood flow is redirected to healthy veins

This procedure effectively shrinks the varicocele and relieves symptoms with minimal discomfort.
