What is Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT)?

Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT) is a condition where a blood clot forms in the deep veins, most commonly in the legs. This can block normal blood flow and cause serious complications if not treated promptly.

If a clot breaks loose and travels to the lungs, it can lead to a life-threatening condition called pulmonary embolism.

Early diagnosis and timely treatment are critical to prevent complications.

Symptoms of DVT

  • Swelling in one leg
  • Pain or tenderness in the leg
  • Warmth in the affected area
  • Red or discolored skin
  • Cramping or soreness in the calf

Some patients may not experience obvious symptoms, making medical evaluation important if risk factors are present.

Causes & Risk Factors

  • Prolonged immobility (bed rest, long travel)
  • Recent surgery or injury
  • Pregnancy
  • Obesity
  • Smoking
  • Hormonal therapy
  • History of blood clotting disorders

Advanced DVT Treatment

Treatment depends on the severity and location of the clot. Interventional Radiology offers minimally invasive options for effective treatment.

  • Catheter-Directed Thrombolysis: Medication delivered directly to dissolve clot
  • Mechanical Thrombectomy: Removal of clot using specialized devices
  • IVC Filter Placement: Prevents clots from reaching lungs

These techniques provide faster relief and reduce long-term complications.

Procedure Overview

  • Performed under imaging guidance
  • Small puncture for catheter insertion
  • Targeted clot removal or dissolution
  • Short hospital stay

Patients benefit from faster recovery and improved outcomes compared to traditional approaches.
