What Are Uterine Fibroids?

Uterine fibroids are non-cancerous growths that develop in or on the walls of the uterus. They are also known as leiomyomas, myomas, or fibromas.

These growths are common in women during reproductive years and may vary in size, number, and location. While most fibroids are harmless, some can cause symptoms that affect quality of life.

Nearly 70–80% of women may develop fibroids by the age of 50, though not all require treatment.

Types of Uterine Fibroids

  • Intramural Fibroids: Grow within the uterine wall (most common)
  • Subserosal Fibroids: Develop on the outer surface of the uterus
  • Submucosal Fibroids: Grow inside the uterine cavity
  • Cervical Fibroids: Located in the cervix
  • Pedunculated Fibroids: Attached by a stalk (inside or outside uterus)

Causes & Risk Factors

The exact cause of fibroids is unknown, but several factors contribute to their development:

  • Hormonal imbalance (estrogen & progesterone)
  • Genetic changes in uterine muscle cells
  • Family history (hereditary factors)
  • Age (common in women 30–50 years)
  • Obesity and lifestyle factors
  • High intake of red meat, alcohol, caffeine

Symptoms of Fibroids

Many women may not experience symptoms, but when present, they may include:

  • Heavy or prolonged menstrual bleeding
  • Pelvic pain or pressure
  • Pain during intercourse
  • Frequent urination
  • Constipation
  • Lower back or leg pain
  • Swollen abdomen
  • Infertility or pregnancy complications

Treatment Options for Fibroids

Treatment depends on the size, location, and severity of symptoms. Options include:

Medications
  • Hormonal therapy (GnRH)
  • Pain relief medications
  • Oral contraceptives
  • Iron supplements for anaemia
Minimally Invasive Treatment

Uterine Fibroid Embolization (UFE) is a highly effective, non-surgical procedure performed by an interventional radiologist.

  • A catheter is inserted through the groin artery
  • Embolic particles are delivered to block blood flow
  • Fibroids shrink over time

Studies show that up to 95% of women experience significant relief from symptoms after this procedure.
